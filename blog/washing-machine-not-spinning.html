Washing Machine Not Spinning? Causes and What to Check

Clothes come out dripping and the drum barely turns. Here is how to narrow down the fault and when to call in a technician.

6 min read Updated October 2026 Written by our repair technicians
Book On-Site Repair Call Emergency Tech

A washing machine that won't spin leaves you with a drum full of heavy, soaking laundry. In many cases the fix is straightforward. In others, a worn part such as a drive belt, motor brushes or a door lock needs replacing.

Quick answer

A washing machine usually won't spin because of an unbalanced or overloaded drum, water that hasn't drained, a faulty door or lid lock, a worn or snapped drive belt, worn motor carbon brushes or a failed motor or control board. Check the load and drain filter first.

Why your washing machine won't spin

1. An unbalanced or overloaded drum

Washers sense an out-of-balance load and will slow down, pause or skip the spin to protect themselves. A single heavy item such as a duvet or a bunched-up towel is a typical trigger.

2. Water that hasn't drained

Most machines won't start spinning until the drum is empty. If the pump is blocked or failing, the spin never begins. See our washing machine not draining guide.

3. A faulty door or lid lock

The machine only spins when it senses the door or lid is safely locked. A worn latch, broken switch or faulty lock will stop the cycle even though everything else works.

4. A worn or snapped drive belt

On belt-driven models, the belt connects the motor to the drum. If it slips or snaps, you may hear the motor running while the drum barely turns, or turns freely by hand with no resistance.

5. Worn motor carbon brushes

Many machines use motors with carbon brushes that wear down over time. Symptoms include weak or slow spinning, intermittent spin and sometimes a burning smell.

6. Motor, coupler or control board faults

A failed motor, a worn motor coupler on top-loaders, a faulty speed sensor or a damaged electronic control board can all stop the drum spinning, often with an error code on the display.

7. Worn drum bearings

Failing bearings cause a rumbling or grinding noise and make the drum feel rough or wobbly when turned by hand. Left alone, they can damage the drum itself.

Quick checks before you call

  1. Rebalance the load. Pause the cycle, redistribute the laundry and remove one or two heavy items, then run a spin-only cycle.
  2. Look for standing water. If water remains in the drum, deal with the drainage problem first. Be careful: it may be hot.
  3. Check the door or lid. Close it firmly and check nothing is trapped in the seal. On top-loaders, make sure the lid is properly aligned and the latch moves freely.
  4. Note any error code. Check the display or your manual for the code and write it down. It narrows the fault considerably for your technician.
  5. Turn the drum by hand. With the machine switched off at the wall, turn the drum. It should turn smoothly. Rumbling or scraping suggests bearings, while spinning freely with almost no resistance suggests a belt or coupler problem.
  6. Check the machine is level and transit bolts are removed. On new or relocated front-loaders, shipping bolts at the back must be removed, and uneven feet cause violent shaking that triggers safety cut-outs.
  7. Reset the machine. Switch it off at the wall for five minutes, then restart. This can clear a temporary electronic fault.

When to call a technician

Do not force the door open

If the drum is full of water, don't force the door. Switch the machine off at the wall, then use the drain filter or emergency drain hose to release the water first. Always unplug before reaching near the pump or motor.

  • The machine still won't spin after rebalancing and checking the drain.
  • You hear a loud grinding or rumbling, or the machine shakes violently.
  • There is a burning smell, or the machine trips your breaker.
  • An error code returns after resetting the machine.
  • The drum won't turn at all, or turns with no resistance.
