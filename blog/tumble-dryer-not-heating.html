Tumble Dryer Not Heating? Causes, Checks & Repairs

The drum turns, but clothes come out damp and cold. Here is what usually causes it, what you can check and why it is important not to ignore.

6 min read Updated October 2026 Written by our repair technicians
Book On-Site Repair Call Emergency Tech

A tumble dryer that spins but doesn't heat is frustrating, especially during the wetter months. The cause is often a blocked lint filter or vent that has overheated the machine, or a part that has failed because of that overheating.

Quick answer

A dryer that won't heat usually has a blocked lint filter or exhaust vent, a blown thermal fuse, a failed heating element or a faulty thermostat. On condenser and heat-pump models, a full water container or clogged condenser or filter can also stop heating. Always find out why the part failed, not just which one.

Why a tumble dryer stops heating

1. A clogged lint filter or blocked vent

Airflow carries heat and moisture out of the drum. When lint blocks the filter or the exhaust vent is crushed or blocked, heat builds up and the dryer's safety cut-outs switch the heating off.

2. A blown thermal fuse or cut-out

The thermal fuse is a one-time safety device that protects against overheating. When it blows, the dryer will often still tumble but won't heat. It usually blows because of a blocked vent or a faulty thermostat.

3. A failed heating element

On vented and many condenser models, an electric element generates the heat. Over time elements burn out, particularly in machines that have run hot because of poor airflow.

4. A faulty thermostat or sensor

Thermostats and temperature sensors switch the element on and off. If they fail, the element may not receive power at all, or may overheat.

5. Condenser and heat-pump specific faults

A full water container, a clogged condenser unit or a dirty heat exchanger reduces drying performance and can trigger cut-outs. Heat-pump dryers also depend on clean filters and heat exchangers to work efficiently.

6. A control board or timer fault

If the electronics don't send power to the heating circuit, the drum will still turn without any heat. This is less common but does happen.

Safe checks you can do at home

  1. Check the program. Make sure you haven't selected an air-only or "no heat" option, or a delicate setting that uses very low heat.
  2. Clean the lint filter. Remove and clean it after every load, and wipe the housing. A filter that looks clean can still be clogged with a film of fabric softener residue, so wash it with warm water occasionally.
  3. Check the vent and hose. On vented dryers, make sure the hose isn't kinked, crushed or full of lint, and that the outside vent opens freely.
  4. Empty the water container. On condenser and heat-pump models, empty the container after every cycle and check the drain line isn't blocked.
  5. Don't overload, and spin clothes well first. Overfilled drums restrict airflow, and very wet clothes take far longer to dry. Use a high spin speed in your washing machine.
  6. Check the power. Make sure the plug is fully in and the breaker hasn't tripped. Heating circuits draw much more power than the drum motor alone.
  7. Clean the condenser or heat exchanger. If your manual shows how to access it, rinse or vacuum it according to the instructions.

When to call a technician

Lint is flammable

Lint build-up combined with an overheating fault is a recognised fire risk. If you smell burning, the drum or door feels unusually hot or the dryer repeatedly cuts out, stop using it and unplug it until it has been inspected.

Book a diagnostic visit if:

  • The dryer still doesn't heat after cleaning the filter and checking the vent.
  • Clothes take two or more cycles to dry.
  • You notice a burning smell, or the machine shuts off mid-cycle.
  • The drum doesn't turn or makes a loud squealing noise (see our tumble dryer repairs).

A technician tests the element, thermostats and thermal fuse with a meter, then finds and fixes the cause of any blockage. Replacing a blown thermal fuse without fixing the underlying problem simply leads to another failure, which is why we diagnose before we replace.
