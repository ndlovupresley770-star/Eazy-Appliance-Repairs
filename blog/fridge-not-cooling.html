Fridge Not Cooling? Causes, Quick Checks & When to Call a Technician

A warm fridge puts your food at risk fast. Here is how to work out what has gone wrong, what you can safely check yourself and when it is time for a professional.

6 min read Updated October 2026 Written by our repair technicians
Book On-Site Repair Call Emergency Tech

When a fridge stops cooling, the clock starts ticking on everything inside it. The good news is that the cause is often something simple. The less good news is that some causes involve sealed-system work that only a qualified technician should touch. This guide helps you tell the difference.

Quick answer

The most common causes are dirty condenser coils, a failed fan motor, a faulty defrost system, a bad thermostat or temperature sensor and a failing compressor start relay. Work through the safe checks below, and call a technician if the fridge is still warm after 24 hours.

Why a fridge stops cooling

Fridges move heat from inside the cabinet to the coils at the back or underneath. Anything that interrupts that cycle will leave the interior warm. These are the usual suspects:

1. Dirty condenser coils

Dust and pet hair insulate the condenser coils, so the fridge can't shed heat. The compressor runs longer, the fridge struggles to reach temperature and the motor wears faster.

2. A failed condenser or evaporator fan

Fans push air across the coils and around the compartments. If a fan stops, you may hear a change in sound, the freezer may stay cold while the fridge warms up, or the back of the fridge may feel unusually hot.

3. Defrost system failure

In frost-free fridges, a faulty defrost heater, timer or sensor lets frost build up over the evaporator and block airflow. A classic sign is a freezer that feels cold while the fridge section above it slowly warms.

4. A faulty thermostat or temperature sensor

If the thermostat or sensor reads incorrectly, the compressor may not start when it should, or it may stop too early.

5. A failed start relay or overload protector

A repetitive click from the back every few minutes usually means the compressor is trying to start and failing. This is a common and often inexpensive fix.

6. Worn door seals

A torn or hardened gasket lets warm, humid air in. The fridge runs constantly but can't keep up, and frost may form near the door.

7. A refrigerant leak or compressor failure

If the compressor runs but the fridge never gets cold, there may be a leak in the sealed system or a failing compressor. This needs specialist pressure testing and gas recharge.

Safe checks you can do at home

Before you call anyone, these checks take a few minutes and rule out the simple causes:

  1. Check the power. Make sure the plug is fully in, the wall socket works (try a lamp) and the circuit breaker hasn't tripped. After a power cut or surge, a fridge can take time to restart.
  2. Check the temperature setting. A knocked dial is surprisingly common. As a guide, aim for about 3–5°C in the fridge and -18°C in the freezer.
  3. Listen and feel. Is the compressor humming at the back? Can you hear the fan inside the freezer? Silence, or a click every few minutes, is useful information to pass on to your technician.
  4. Clear the airflow. Overpacked shelves and food pressed against the back wall block the vents that circulate cold air. Leave a small gap around items.
  5. Test the door seals. Close the door on a strip of paper. If it slides out easily, the seal isn't gripping and may need replacing.
  6. Clean the condenser coils. Switch the fridge off at the wall, then use a vacuum with a brush attachment on the coils at the back or underneath. Many modern fridges have no exposed coils, so check your manual.
  7. Give it 24 hours. After any change, leave the fridge running with the doors closed. A fridge can take many hours to return to temperature.

When to stop and call a technician

Never attempt gas or sealed-system work

Many modern fridges use R600a (isobutane), which is flammable. Regassing, leak repair and compressor replacement must only be done by a trained technician with the right equipment.

Book a diagnostic visit if any of these apply:

  • The fridge is still warm 24 hours after the simple checks.
  • You hear repeated clicking, or the compressor never starts or runs non-stop.
  • Heavy frost or ice is forming on the back wall or in the freezer.
  • There is a burning smell, or the plug or socket is hot.
  • Water is pooling around the base of the fridge (see our fridge leaking water guide).

Our technician carries out electrical and pressure tests on-site, identifies the failed component and gives you a fixed quote before starting. Most fridge repairs are completed in a single visit, using genuine spare parts carried on the van.

Protecting your food in the meantime

As general food-safety guidance, an unopened fridge keeps food cold for roughly four hours after the power or cooling is lost. A full freezer holds its temperature for about 48 hours (about 24 hours if half full) if the door stays closed.

  • Keep the doors closed as much as possible.
  • Move essentials such as milk, meat and medicines to a cooler bag with ice.
  • If in doubt, throw perishable food out when it has been above about 5°C for more than two hours.
